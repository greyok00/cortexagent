WHERE BIKE
DASHING WORKS

Two wheels tend to get framed as a downtown-only thing, not for residential areas. It works in both places, for different reasons.

Dense cities

Parking is often the slowest part of a car delivery. On a bike there's no circling the block, no garage, no meter. Bike lanes stay open when traffic doesn't, and a bike can get to the merchant door and the customer's building directly.

Neighborhoods

Residential runs are usually short. A bike covers a few blocks quickly, and door-to-door is faster when the drop-off is a house or a low-rise rather than a tower with a loading zone.

As a Bike Dasher, you'll receive priority for short-distance, high-demand deliveries in busy city zones.

COSTS OF DELIVERING
WITH YOUR BIKE

A car brings running costs that a bike doesn't have at all.

NO FUEL COSTS

$0. Stop-and-go city driving is where fuel costs add up fastest, and that whole line disappears on a bike.

NO INSURANCE AND REGISTRATION COSTS

$0. Bike and eBike dashing doesn't require auto insurance or vehicle registration, so there's nothing to carry or renew.*

LOWER MAINTENANCE COSTS

Regular bike upkeep instead of oil changes, transmission work, and everything else a motor needs.

CHOOSING BETWEEN A
BIKE AND AN EBIKE

Both are vehicle types that you can use to dash where they're available*. The difference is range, upfront cost, and local rules, not eligibility.

*eBike rules are set locally, so requirements vary by city and state.

RANGE AND TERRAIN

A motor takes the hills and the headwind out of it, which matters most on a long dash or in a city that isn't flat. A regular bike covers a compact, flat zone without any of that.

UPFRONT COST

Many Dashers already own a bike. An eBike is a purchase or a rental, which is where the partner discounts below come in.

LOCAL RULES

eBikes are regulated by city and state: battery certification, speed limits, and helmet.
